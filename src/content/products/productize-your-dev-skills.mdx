reframe: |-
  You can build websites, applications, integrations, automations, and other technical solutions. Then a prospect asks what you do, and the answer becomes a list of technologies followed by “it depends on what you need.” That sounds flexible, but it leaves the buyer to define the commercial job.

  The same uncertainty follows the rest of the sale. Each inquiry can change the buyer, problem, scope, proof, process, and definition of done. Pricing gets harder because there is no stable service underneath the number. Delivery lessons are harder to reuse because too many parts of the engagement change at once.

  Productizing the service means deciding which parts should stop changing. You identify a buyer and problem worth testing, define what the client is actually paying you to deliver, make the boundaries visible, keep professional judgment where it belongs, and give material uncertainty a discovery or separate-scope route before it becomes a promise.

  - question: Do I have to choose one niche forever?

  - question: What if my work is too unpredictable for a fixed package?
    answer: You do not have to force it into one. The guide separates stable work from bounded variation, professional judgment, and material unknowns. Depending on what you find, the responsible offer may be productized implementation, paid discovery, a narrow component, custom work, or no offer yet.

    answer: Yes, if you can already deliver the technical work competently. You can use employment, agency, subcontracting, open-source, serious personal-product, and demonstration work as capability evidence when you describe it accurately. You still need outside evidence before treating buyer demand as established.
