reframe: |-
  A buyer asks, “What would something like this cost?” The tempting response is to start calculating immediately: estimate the build hours, check a familiar rate, look at what other freelancers charge, react to the buyer's budget, add a buffer, and choose a number that feels plausible.

  The problem is not that any one of those inputs is useless. The problem is that they are being asked to answer different questions at the same time.

  Your business economics tell you what the business must support. The Scope Record defines the obligation. The Whole-Engagement Estimate forecasts the provider effort. The Uncertainty Review shows what can and cannot be responsibly committed to. The Pricing Model decides how that uncertainty should be allocated. The Project Economic Floor tests the engagement against your internal economics. Commercial Context helps you judge the client-facing price above that floor. Boundaries and payment structure protect the accepted deal. Delivery evidence tells you what should change next time.

  Web Developer Pricing System teaches that complete chain. The goal is not a universal rate, markup, value percentage, or market-price table. It is a repeatable way to make a pricing decision, explain why the commercial structure fits the work, and improve the system as real projects create better evidence.

faqs:
  - question: Does this tell me what hourly rate to charge?
    answer: No. It calculates a Minimum Sustainable Rate from your own Annual Revenue Requirement and realistic Revenue-Producing Capacity. The MSR is an internal planning threshold used inside later project decisions, not automatically a public billing rate or the price of a project.
  - question: Do I have to use fixed pricing?
    answer: No. Fixed price is appropriate only when the obligation and remaining uncertainty support it. The system also covers T&M, capped T&M, paid discovery, hybrid structures, recurring arrangements when a real recurring obligation exists, and situations where the work should be reshaped, deferred, or declined.
  - question: What if I cannot estimate the project accurately yet?
    answer: Do not hide a material unknown inside a confident-looking fixed number. The system shows how to verify cheap unknowns, narrow scope, use paid discovery, use T&M or capped T&M, split the work into phases, or choose another structure that fits what can actually be known.
  - question: Is this value pricing?
    answer: No percentage-of-value formula is used. Problem importance, buyer alternatives, cost of delay, relevant proof, and other buyer context can inform Commercial Context, but they sit alongside your economics, uncertainty, capacity, procurement constraints, and market plausibility. The guide does not ask you to invent ROI you do not control.
  - question: What is the difference between the Project Economic Floor and the Commercial Price?
    answer: The Project Economic Floor is an internal constraint calculated from expected total effort, the current MSR, and direct project costs. The Commercial Price is the client-facing price chosen later after the engagement, uncertainty, Pricing Model, floor, and Commercial Context have been considered.
  - question: Does the system stop once the client accepts the price?
    answer: No. After delivery, you compare estimate with actual effort, diagnose the mechanism behind meaningful variance, calculate Realized Revenue per Actual Effort Hour as a diagnostic, and make the smallest policy change the evidence supports.
