<ProductSection eyebrow="Prospecting should not start from zero every morning" title="The hard part is not finding businesses. It is deciding which ones actually deserve an email.">

You can ask AI for a list of companies, inspect a few websites, and write five emails today. Tomorrow you still have to remember what you searched, which businesses you reviewed, what evidence you trusted, who you contacted, which follow-up is due, whether anyone replied, and who must never be contacted again.

That is why prospecting becomes inconsistent. The individual tasks are manageable. **The missing piece is an operating system holding them together.**

AI Prospecting Starter turns the work into a repeatable process you can inspect, trust, and run again even when client work gets busy.

</ProductSection>

<ProductSection eyebrow="Do not automate the mistake" title="A faster bad prospecting process is still a bad prospecting process." tone="muted">

The obvious temptation is to automate first: find fifty companies, force AI to identify a problem with every website, enrich every contact, generate all the emails, and schedule the sequence.

That sounds efficient until the system confidently tells an owner about placeholder text that is not on the site, a broken form that works normally, a performance problem you never measured, or a domain that does not belong to the business you meant to contact.

Then automation has not saved you time. It has scaled an evidence problem.

**Tier 1 takes the opposite approach: first make the decisions reliable, then automate the repetition.**

</ProductSection>

<ProductSection eyebrow="The operating system" title="Every lead passes through the same gates in the same order.">

**Discover -> Verify Business -> Inspect Current Website -> Qualify or Reject -> Find/Verify Contact -> Draft from Approved Evidence -> Pre-Send Gate -> Send -> Follow Up -> Stop/Record -> Measure**

Discovery can be broad. Qualification is intentionally strict. Contact enrichment happens only after the business is worth contacting. Copy can use only approved evidence. Every send, reply, bounce, opt-out, and stop event is recorded before the next action happens.

The system can reject every business in a batch. That is a feature, not a failure.

A business does not qualify because the site looks old, a search snippet hints at a problem, or an enrichment tool found the owner's email. It qualifies when the business fits the campaign **and** you can verify a current issue your service can reasonably improve.

</ProductSection>

<ProductSection eyebrow="Stop selling from opinions" title="Separate what you observed from what you think it means." tone="muted">

A useful finding has layers.

**Observed fact:** a current Request an Estimate button leads to a missing page.

**Interpretation:** someone trying to request an estimate may hit a dead end.

**Sales relevance:** repairing that conversion path is within your service.

**Outreach wording:** mention the broken path and the plausible customer consequence—nothing more.

The guide gives you a simple test for every proposed claim: **if the owner replies, “Where exactly did you see that?” can you answer with a current URL and a clear factual observation?** If not, do not use it.

</ProductSection>

<ProductSection eyebrow="A tracker that actually remembers" title="Your spreadsheet becomes the durable state of the prospecting system.">

Chat history is not a database, and a list of names and emails is not enough.

The included **Lead Engine Campaign Tracker** records Campaign definitions, current Lead state, approved and rejected Findings, exact evidence URLs, contact-source information, Activity history, follow-up timing, replies, bounces, opt-outs, Suppression, and weekly Metrics.

That lets you answer the safety questions before every send: Have we researched this domain already? Which finding was approved? Was a message actually sent? Has the prospect replied? Is the address suppressed? Which follow-up is really due?

</ProductSection>

<ProductSection eyebrow="Outreach comes after qualification" title="Do not spend credits—or create temptation—on companies you should reject." tone="muted">

Once a business passes the evidence gate, you work through a contact-sourcing hierarchy. Start with the company's own site, then credible official profiles and directories. Record where the address came from. If no public address exists, Hunter or Apollo can help after the company is otherwise worth contacting.

What you do not do is invent likely addresses and test them by sending.

The first email is intentionally short: why this business is receiving the message, one verified observation, the plausible customer or business consequence, and one easy CTA. No fake familiarity, invented metrics, fearmongering, or unsolicited five-page audit.

Before Gmail opens, the pre-send gate checks the state again. A scheduled follow-up date never overrides a reply, opt-out, hard bounce, duplicate step, stale finding, or campaign cap.

</ProductSection>

<ProductSection eyebrow="A habit you can keep running" title="Thirty to sixty focused minutes beats another burst-and-abandon cycle.">

The daily routine begins with inbound state, not new lead discovery. Review replies. Update suppression. Process due follow-ups. Then research a small batch, reject weak evidence, verify contacts for qualified businesses, draft from approved findings, send within the cap, and log every external action.

The weekly review looks at qualification rate, valid-contact rate, bounce rate, reply rate, positive replies, consultations, closes, and the lessons hiding inside rejected leads and negative responses.

The guide includes a complete five-candidate example, troubleshooting matrix, prompt pack, tracker data dictionary, and a **14-day Tier 1 launch plan** so you do not have to infer how the pieces fit.

</ProductSection>
