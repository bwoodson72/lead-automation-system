  The call felt good. The buyer seemed interested. Then you hear the sentence that sounds like progress: “Send me a proposal.” You disappear for a few hours, turn notes into scope, make the document look professional, hit send, and suddenly the whole deal is on the other side of an inbox you cannot control.

  Then the questions arrive late. Can we add this? Can you sharpen the price? Who else needs to approve it? What happens if we change direction? Sometimes nothing arrives at all. A verbal yes becomes a week of checking email because nobody defined what decision was actually supposed to happen next.

You confirm the opportunity is ready, translate discovery into a bounded outcome and scope, make uncertainty and assumptions visible, carry in a price and payment policy, present around a real decision event, classify revisions, and define Closed Won as agreement plus required payment. The proposal becomes a record of a deal taking shape, not a document you throw over the wall and hope comes back signed.

    steps:
      - label: Gate the opportunity
        value: The owner asks for a proposal, but the developer does not start writing. Authority, investment range, timing, dependencies, risk, and a Tuesday review with the owner and marketing manager are established before the opportunity is marked proposal-ready.
      - label: Build the commercial baseline
        value: The scope becomes 10 marketing pages, a CMS, inquiry forms, up to 25 migrated items, five analytics events, two design revision rounds, explicit client responsibilities, and exclusions. The unverified CRM integration is separated into a $750 assessment instead of hidden inside the base quote.
      - label: Price and propose
        value: The defined project is priced at $8,500 with a private $7,700 walk-away for the same scope. The proposal follows a nine-section decision structure and initially uses 40/30/30 payment triggers tied to booking, design approval, and pre-launch.
      - label: Diagnose the objection
        value: When the owner says the price is higher than expected, the developer learns that $7,000 came from a narrower competitor quote and that the bigger concern is paying half upfront. The response separates scope comparison from cash-flow timing instead of discounting identical work.
      - label: Execute before Closed Won
        value: The $8,500 scope stays intact while payment timing changes to 35/35/30. Redwood is marked Accepted / Pending Execution until the final SOW is checked, the agreement is signed, and the $2,975 initial payment is received. Only then is the start date reserved.
