reframe: |-
  Technical range is useful. Commercial ambiguity is not.

  You may be able to build websites, applications, integrations, automations, internal tools, and custom software. But when a buyer asks what you sell and the answer is a list of capabilities followed by “it depends,” the buyer still has to figure out what problem you are actually taking responsibility for.

  That ambiguity follows the money. Marketing stays generic because the audience and problem keep changing. Pricing gets harder because there is no stable obligation underneath the number. Sales conversations become requirements sessions. Proof is difficult to reuse because every project is framed differently. Delivery lessons do not compound because too many parts of the engagement change at once.

  Productizing does not mean forcing custom development into a rigid package. It means deciding which parts of the commercial offer should stop changing. You choose a buyer and problem worth testing, define the outcome and boundaries, preserve judgment where it matters, route material uncertainty into discovery or separate scope, and build proof around a service the market can actually evaluate.

  Productize Your Dev Skills helps turn technical ability into a commercial object: something you can explain consistently, price more deliberately, market to a recognizable buyer, and improve from evidence instead of reinventing it for every inquiry.

    answer: You do not have to force it into one. The guide separates stable work from bounded variation, professional judgment, and material unknowns.     answer: Yes, if you can already deliver the technical work competently. You can use employment, agency, subcontracting, open-source, serious personal-product, and demonstration work as capability evidence when you describe it accurately.
