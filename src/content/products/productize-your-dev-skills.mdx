reframe: |-
  A prospect asks a simple question: “So what exactly do you do?” If the answer is React, APIs, integrations, automation, performance, custom development, and “it depends on what you need,” you have handed the buyer a job. They now have to decide which capability matters, what problem you should solve, what the engagement looks like, and why you are worth more than another flexible developer.

  That uncertainty follows you everywhere. Every inquiry becomes a fresh discovery exercise. Every proposal starts from a blank page. Scope changes with the person in front of you. Pricing feels harder because there is no stable thing being priced. The flexibility that makes you useful during delivery can make you look interchangeable before the sale.

  Productizing does not mean turning yourself into a template shop or pretending every project is identical. It means deciding what should stop changing. You choose a reachable buyer, a recurring problem, a useful outcome, clear boundaries, repeatable delivery steps, and proof that makes the offer believable. You keep the technical judgment. You stop making the commercial side of the business up from scratch.
