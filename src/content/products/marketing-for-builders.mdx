reframe: |-
  Marketing is the work that makes the business easier to find, understand, trust, and remember before a sales conversation begins.

  That matters because technical quality is mostly invisible to a buyer at first. They do not see your codebase, architecture decisions, debugging ability, or delivery discipline when they are deciding whom to contact. They see a market position, a message, proof, a website, a recommendation, a search result, a post, a referral, or some other surface that tells them whether you appear relevant.

  When those surfaces are weak, the business pays for it in demand. The pipeline gets quiet and marketing turns into emergency activity: rewrite the site, post more, start SEO, join a community, launch a newsletter, try another channel. Client work returns and the activity stops. Because the underlying decisions were never connected, the next quiet period begins with the same question.

  Marketing for Builders gives those decisions one operating system. You choose a market worth focused attention, decide what a qualified buyer needs to understand and believe, connect claims to proof, build a small set of buyer-facing resources, choose access routes that fit your capacity, and measure evidence that can distinguish a market problem from a message, proof, access, or execution problem.

  The goal is not to become a full-time marketer. It is to create enough consistent demand infrastructure that good work has a better chance of being seen, understood, and considered by people who can actually buy it.

faqs:
  - question: Is this mainly a content-marketing guide?
    answer: No. Content comes after more basic decisions. The system first establishes who you are trying to become relevant to, what makes the work matter, what a buyer should understand and believe, and what evidence supports that argument. Content is one way to carry that argument, not the strategy itself.
  - question: How is this different from Client Acquisition Without Upwork?
    answer: Client Acquisition Without Upwork is about creating and managing individual opportunities across buyer-access channels. Marketing for Builders develops the market understanding, commercial argument, proof, buyer-facing infrastructure, access routes, and management loop that make you easier to understand, evaluate, encounter, and remember over time.
  - question: Do I need to choose one permanent niche before I can use this?
    answer: No. The book teaches a market hypothesis, not a niche oath. You choose a direction specific enough to investigate, record why it is reasonable, and write down the evidence that would make you narrow, broaden, change, or drop it.
  - question: What if I do not have strong case studies or quantified client outcomes?
    answer: The system does not tell you to invent them. You learn to match claims to the evidence you actually have, including technical artifacts, demonstrations, process evidence, project records, verified delivered states, and client evidence where it legitimately exists.
  - question: Will I have to post every day or follow a fixed marketing calendar?
    answer: No. The operating section starts from your real delivery capacity and builds a rhythm that can survive client work. Routes, maintenance obligations, activation, and review are kept deliberately bounded so marketing does not become a second full-time job.
  - question: What does the workbook actually do?
    answer: The 20 modules turn the guide into an operating record. You build and update a Market Model, Market Argument, Market Access record, and Marketing Management record, then carry those decisions forward instead of starting from a blank page every time you change a message, asset, channel, or experiment.
