reframe: |-
  Agency partnerships can be economically attractive for a simple reason: one relationship can generate several paid projects without forcing you to source and close every end client yourself.

  That can reduce acquisition effort, smooth parts of the pipeline, and create repeat demand. It can also tempt you to give away too much before the relationship has earned it.

  Future volume sounds valuable, so you discount. Unpaid presales work expands. Incomplete briefs become your estimating problem. Rush requests become normal. Client-facing authority gets blurry. Capacity is held without commitment. Eventually one partner is large enough that saying no feels dangerous even when the economics are no longer good.

  Agency Partnership Playbook treats the relationship as a business channel that has to prove itself. You form a limited demand hypothesis from public evidence, qualify the agency, discover the operating model, run a bounded paid pilot, control scope and authority during delivery, measure the real cost to serve, then decide whether the evidence supports project-by-project work, a deeper partnership, reserved capacity, repricing, reset, reduction, or exit.

  The objective is not more agency logos. It is repeat paid work that improves the business after coordination, risk, capacity, and concentration are counted.
