reframe: |-
  A prospect is interested enough to give you an hour. That should be the moment the opportunity gets clearer. Instead, it is often the moment a technically capable developer starts solving before the sale is understood.

  The buyer asks how you would build it, how long it might take, what stack you would use, and what something like that costs. You answer because you know how. The conversation feels useful. Then comes the familiar request: "Can you send me a proposal?"

  The problem is that a good technical conversation is not automatically a mature sales conversation. You may still not know why the project matters now, who can approve it, which unknowns could change the scope or estimate, whether the economics fit, what the buyer is comparing you against, or what decision has to happen next.

  That uncertainty has a cost. It turns proposals into research documents, pricing into guesswork, follow-up into chasing, and objections into pressure to discount before you know what the buyer actually means. You can spend hours on an opportunity that was never ready, or win work that should have been reshaped, investigated, or declined.

  You do not need more charisma or a pile of clever questions. You need judgment. You need to know what must be understood before you recommend, which evidence changes the deal, and what the responsible next move is when the opportunity is not ready.

  The Developer Sales Playbook gives you that process. You learn to understand before you recommend, diagnose the business problem without manufacturing pain, expose technical and commercial uncertainty, test mutual fit, connect the recommendation to confirmed needs, work through resistance without canned rebuttals, and decide whether the opportunity has actually earned proposal work.

  The goal is not to force more people to say yes. The goal is to stop guessing what a serious buyer conversation means and start leaving it with a decision you can defend.

faqs:
  - question: Is this a sales-script pack?
    answer: No. You get a process for making better sales decisions, not lines to memorize. The playbook teaches what must be understood before you recommend, which evidence changes the answer, how to follow the buyer's response without losing the purpose of the conversation, and how to review your own behavior afterward.
  - question: Do I need to be naturally good at sales?
    answer: No. The method does not depend on charisma or pressure. It depends on preparation, listening, diagnosis, fit, explicit next decisions, and behavior you can review and improve. You do need to be willing to ask business questions instead of retreating into technical explanation whenever the conversation becomes uncomfortable.
  - question: Can I use this on my next sales call, or do I need to finish all 353 pages first?
    answer: You do not need to finish the entire book before using it. The system is cumulative, but the early chapters and matching workbook modules immediately improve preparation, conversation structure, and the quality of the evidence you collect. As you work through the rest, you add diagnosis, qualification, recommendation, commercial alignment, resistance handling, and Proposal Readiness.
  - question: What if I am already comfortable talking to prospects?
    answer: Comfort is useful, but it is not the same as control. This is most valuable when conversations feel natural but still produce vague next steps, speculative proposals, uncertain commercial fit, or inconsistent decisions. If your current process already gives you reliable diagnosis, qualification, next-step control, and proposal readiness, the playbook may be more depth than you need.
  - question: How do I know when to send the proposal?
    answer: The nine-dimension Proposal Readiness Gate checks Problem Clarity, Business Importance, Solution Fit, Scope / Delivery Clarity, Economic Fit, Decision Authority, Stakeholder Clarity, Timing / Decision Process, and Mutual Commitment. Each condition is Clear, Unresolved, or Blocking. A proposal is justified only when no blocking condition remains and material uncertainty has been resolved or responsibly bounded.
  - question: Does this teach me how to write and negotiate the proposal?
    answer: No. This product deliberately stops at Proposal Ready. It teaches you how to mature the opportunity until proposal work is justified and what information should be handed forward. The Developer Proposal & Closing Kit handles final scope and acceptance criteria, final pricing and payment structure, the buyer-facing proposal, proposal review, downstream negotiation, agreement, payment, and the start gate.
  - question: Do I need the Freelance Developer Launch Kit first?
    answer: No. If you can already deliver a credible service and sales is the bottleneck you clearly need to fix, the Sales Playbook is designed to support a competent first execution on its own. Use the Launch Kit instead when several parts of the business are still unclear and you need the broader end-to-end operating system.
  - question: What happens when the prospect is not ready or not a fit?
    answer: The system does not force every conversation toward a proposal. Depending on the evidence, the responsible outcome may be more discovery, Paid Discovery, Reshape, Defer, Nurture, Refer, or Decline. A clean no can be better sales execution than a proposal built on weak fit.
