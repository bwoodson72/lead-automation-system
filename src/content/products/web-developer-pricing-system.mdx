reframe: |-
  A prospect asks what a project will cost. You estimate the development work, think about what you have charged before, look at what other developers seem to charge, or react to the client's budget. Then you settle on a number that feels reasonable.

  The problem is that the number may be arriving before the project has really been understood. How much work are you actually agreeing to? How much time will go into planning, communication, revisions, testing, launch, and handoff? Which dependencies are still uncertain? What happens if those unknowns turn out to be larger than expected?

  There is also a business question underneath the project question: what does this engagement need to support for taking it on to make sense? If that stays hidden, you can win the work and discover later that the job was underpriced.

  The goal is not simply to quote higher. It is to understand the deal well enough to price it deliberately.
