reframe: |-
  Freelancing can become an exhausting series of local fixes. Rewrite the website because leads are weak. Change the rate because a project was underpriced. Try outreach because referrals slowed down. Build a proposal template because the last deal got messy. Each fix can be sensible and still leave the business feeling fragile.

  The reason is dependency. Pricing is harder when the offer is vague. Outreach is harder when the buyer is broad. Sales calls drift when the problem is not defined. Delivery gets painful when scope and onboarding are improvised. Referrals and retainers remain accidental when the project simply ends. A weakness at one stage keeps leaking into the next.

  The Freelance Developer Launch Kit connects those stages in dependency order. You build enough clarity in market, offer, pricing, proof, acquisition, sales, onboarding, delivery, referrals, recurring value, cash visibility, and weekly management for the next stage to stand on something solid. The goal is not a perfect business before you sell. It is a business with a shape, one you can inspect and improve from evidence instead of rebuilding from memory whenever something goes wrong.

    answer: No. The 13-week calendar deliberately overlaps building and selling. Real buyer contact starts early enough to test the market and offer while the rest of the operating system is still being completed.

  - question: How is this different from buying the individual guides?
    answer: The Launch Kit is the minimum complete business system and emphasizes dependencies between subsystems. The focused products are for deeper work after evidence identifies a specific constraint such as pricing, demand, sales, proposals, agency partnerships, or recurring revenue.

    answer: No. It also fits experienced developers whose business grew through referrals, marketplaces, or accumulated habits and now needs an explicit operating structure.
  - question: Does it cover recurring revenue?
    answer: Yes, but it does not force a retainer onto every client. Recurring work is introduced only after delivery exposes a legitimate ongoing responsibility, output, support need, or capacity requirement.
