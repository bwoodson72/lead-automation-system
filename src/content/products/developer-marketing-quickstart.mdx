reframe: |-
  You know the moment. The project is almost finished, the client is happy, and the invoice is going out. Instead of enjoying the win, part of your attention is already on the empty space behind it. No promising calls. No proposals moving. No pipeline because prospecting stopped when delivery got busy.

  That is when “I need more clients” turns into a scramble. You check Upwork again. You think about posting more. You send a few cold emails. You ask for referrals. Any one of those channels can work, but changing tactics under pressure makes it almost impossible to learn what is actually creating opportunities.

  Developer Marketing Quickstart gives the problem an order. In seven days, you choose one reachable buyer, one problem worth testing, one offer, one message, one channel, and a first group of real prospects. It does not promise that 25 contacts will produce a client. It gives you something more useful than another burst of activity: a small acquisition system you can run, measure, and improve before the calendar becomes scary again.

  system:
    title: One minimum viable customer-acquisition loop from market choice to the next experiment.
    intro: The sequence matters because later tactics cannot repair an earlier decision that was never made. The guide keeps each stage narrow enough to execute, then uses real buyer response to make the next pass smarter.
    steps:
      - Choose one identifiable buyer and confirm you can find at least 25 real prospects or users
      - Find one painful problem using buyer language, consequences, desired outcome, evidence, and current workarounds
      - Turn the problem into an offer with outcome, mechanism, scope, proof, pricing approach, risk reduction, and next step
      - Create a buyer-first message using relevance, problem, consequence, outcome, proof, and CTA
      - Choose one primary acquisition channel for 30 days based on buyer behavior and reachability
      - Source and qualify the First 25 using real evidence and a defensible contact route
      - Run the first batch or direct conversations and record every response, objection, next action, and result
      - Read the funnel like an engineer and identify the weakest stage rather than labeling the whole experiment a failure
      - Change one meaningful variable when practical and schedule the next execution block
