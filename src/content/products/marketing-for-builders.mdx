reframe: |-
  You have probably seen it happen. You land on another developer's site and the work itself does not look better than yours. The technical decisions are not more sophisticated. The portfolio may even be thinner. But the offer is clear, the proof has context, the language sounds like the buyer's problem, and the whole business looks expensive.

  That stings because developers are trained to believe competence should speak for itself. It does not. A buyer cannot inspect the architecture choices you make, the bugs you prevent, the tradeoffs you understand, or the years of judgment behind a good technical decision. They can only judge the evidence you put in front of them.

  Marketing for Builders helps the market see more of what is already there. You decide what you want to be known for, translate technical capability into buyer meaning, build proof without inventing results, make the website answer the buyer's risk questions, publish around problems buyers already feel, and distribute the work where those buyers can actually encounter it. You do not need to become a full-time creator. You need a small marketing system that keeps reducing buyer uncertainty while you continue doing client work.
