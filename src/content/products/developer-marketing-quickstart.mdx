reframe: |-
  Most technical builders reach for tactics too early. You start posting, rewrite the homepage, search marketplaces, send a few messages, ask for referrals, or think about automation before the basic decisions underneath those tactics are stable enough to test.

  That creates activity without a learning loop. If the market is vague, the problem becomes vague. If the problem is vague, the offer becomes generic. If the offer is generic, the message becomes weak. Adding more channels only distributes the uncertainty faster.

  Developer Marketing Quickstart gives customer acquisition an order: Market -> Problem -> Offer -> Message -> Channel -> Action -> Measurement. You make one defensible decision at a time, carry each decision into the next worksheet, put the system in front of real people, and record what happens. The goal is not to prove a market in seven days or guarantee a client from twenty-five prospects. The goal is to leave week one with a live acquisition loop that can teach you what deserves another test.

proof:
  title: The difference is not more activity. It is knowing what the activity is testing.
  body: |-
    The Quickstart turns vague acquisition pressure into a connected set of decisions. The buyer definition determines what you research. The research shapes the problem. The problem shapes the offer and provisional price. The offer gives the message something concrete to say. The buyer and offer make the channel easier to choose. The channel determines how you build and work the First 25. The tracker turns action into evidence.

    steps:
      - label: Choose a market you can investigate
        value: Maya compares three candidate markets on pain, ability to pay, reachability, frequency or urgency, and existing spending. She chooses independent roofing companies because she can identify real businesses, inspect the customer path, and get to evidence quickly.
      - label: Research the problem before selling it
        value: She records public observations, qualitative language, and a contradictory example. Instead of claiming that roofers have bad websites, she narrows the problem to cases where services, proof, and the estimate path are hard for a comparison shopper to follow.
      - label: Make the offer economically usable
        value: Maya turns the desired outcome into a bounded service, derives a fictional $100 minimum effective delivery-hour floor from her own business requirements, estimates the defined project burden, and forms a $6,000 provisional fixed-scope quote. The numbers illustrate the method, not a recommended rate.
      - label: Choose one channel and build the First 25
        value: Targeted outreach wins the first channel comparison because it creates faster qualified feedback. Maya then scores real prospects, identifies relevant contacts, verifies the route, and moves the strongest names to Priority A rather than treating every company on a list as a prospect.
      - label: Work the list in reviewable batches
        value: Maya plans the initial message, first follow-up, final close-the-loop note, and stop rule before she sends. She begins with a small Priority A batch, records exact buyer language, follows legitimate routing, and does not scale a bad assumption just because she planned to contact 25 names.
      - label: Diagnose without pretending the sample is a benchmark
        value: In the illustrative first cycle, 25 qualified prospects produce 23 delivered messages, 4 replies, 1 positive response, 1 call, and no proposal. Maya treats those numbers as locations to inspect, reads repeated buyer language, and changes one meaningful variable while keeping the buyer and channel stable.
    conclusion: The proof is continuity. Each worksheet produces an input the next step actually uses, and the final diagnostic review tells the reader what the current evidence supports, what remains unknown, and which one variable deserves the next test.

    title: Seven stages, eight implementation chapters, one acquisition loop.

      - Diagnose mechanics, stage counts, buyer language, and comparison before changing one meaningful variable
