reframe: |-
  A monthly invoice can make revenue more predictable while making the work less predictable. If the client is paying for “ongoing help,” every request becomes arguable, every quiet month creates doubt, every urgent request pressures the relationship, and reserved capacity quietly competes with project work.

  The Freelancer Retainer System treats recurring service as a product decision before it becomes a billing decision. You first prove that a real recurring need exists. Then you choose the model that matches what the client is actually buying: maintenance, support, planned growth, reserved capacity, managed responsibility, or a deliberately bounded hybrid.

  From there you define covered systems, included obligations, limits, project triggers, support and response rules, request flow, overage, rollover, capacity, pricing, onboarding, monthly planning, reporting, renewal, concentration, and exit. The finish line is not “understand retainers.” It is a recurring-service operating system another competent person could read and run without inventing the rules month by month.

faqs:
  - question: Should every completed project become a retainer?
    answer: No. The first decision is whether a recurring need actually exists and whether the relationship, economics, boundaries, and capacity support it. Project-only is a legitimate outcome.
  - question: Is a retainer just prepaid hours?
    answer: Not necessarily. The guide distinguishes purchased labor from reserved capacity, maintenance stewardship, support response, growth output, and managed responsibility. The economic product determines rollover, utilization expectations, pricing, and reporting.
  - question: What if the client barely uses the service?
    answer: Underuse is a health signal, not a reason to invent busywork. Review whether the client is buying stewardship or reserved availability legitimately, whether the service should be reduced or restructured, or whether the recurring relationship should end.
  - question: What if the client uses too much?
    answer: Requests are classified before scheduling, capacity has measurable limits, and the overage framework gives the client choices such as defer, reprioritize, approve additional work, or move the request into a separate project.
  - question: Does this cover renewals and exits?
    answer: Yes. Renewal is treated as a product-fit review with explicit options to renew unchanged, expand, reduce, restructure, or end. The system also covers notice, handoff, failed payment, suspension, reactivation, and when response obligations stop.
  - question: What is in the workbook?
    answer: The 65-page workbook contains 27 RT-coded tools covering fit, model design, scope, economics, capacity, response, requests, overage, rollover, onboarding, baseline, monthly operation, reporting, health, renewal, failed payment, portfolio risk, proposal, the final one-page operating system, and the 30-day launch plan.
