reframe: |-
  Getting a serious prospect into a conversation is expensive enough that the opportunity deserves more than a technically helpful chat.

  Yet this is where many developers lose commercial control. The buyer asks how you would build the solution, which stack you would use, how long it might take, and what something like that costs. You answer because you know the technical work. The call feels productive. Then the buyer asks for a proposal.

  A useful technical conversation can still be an immature sales conversation. You may not know why the project matters now, what the buyer is trying to change, who can approve the work, which unknowns could materially change scope or price, whether the economics fit, what alternatives the buyer is considering, or what decision needs to happen next.

  That gap costs time and revenue opportunity. Proposals become unpaid discovery documents. Pricing is forced onto incomplete information. Follow-up turns into chasing. Objections trigger defensive explanations or discounts before you know what the buyer actually means. Sometimes the work closes and turns out to have been a poor commercial fit from the beginning.

  Developer Sales Playbook gives the conversation a job: reduce enough uncertainty for both sides to make the next responsible commercial decision. You learn to understand the business problem before recommending, expose technical and commercial unknowns, test mutual fit, connect the recommendation to confirmed needs, establish broad economic alignment, work through resistance without canned rebuttals, and decide whether the opportunity has actually earned proposal work.

  The purpose is not to pressure more people into saying yes. It is to convert good opportunities more deliberately, stop wasting sales effort on weak ones, and move the right buyers toward a clear decision you can build a profitable engagement around.

faqs:
  - question: Is this a sales-script pack?
    answer: No. You get a process for making better sales decisions, not lines to memorize. The playbook teaches what must be understood before you recommend, which evidence changes the answer, how to follow the buyer's response without losing the purpose of the conversation, and how to review your own behavior afterward.
  - question: Do I need to be naturally good at sales?
    answer: No. The method does not depend on charisma or pressure. It depends on preparation, listening, diagnosis, fit, explicit next decisions, and behavior you can review and improve. You do need to be willing to ask business questions instead of retreating into technical explanation whenever the conversation becomes uncomfortable.
  - question: Can I use this on my next sales call, or do I need to finish all 353 pages first?
    answer: You do not need to finish the entire book before using it. The system is cumulative, but the early chapters and matching workbook modules immediately improve preparation, conversation structure, and the quality of the evidence you collect. As you work through the rest, you add diagnosis, qualification, recommendation, commercial alignment, resistance handling, and Proposal Readiness.
  - question: What if I am already comfortable talking to prospects?
    answer: Comfort is useful, but it is not the same as commercial control. This is most valuable when conversations feel natural but still produce vague next steps, speculative proposals, uncertain fit, or inconsistent decisions. If your current process already gives you reliable diagnosis, qualification, next-step control, and proposal readiness, the playbook may be more depth than you need.
  - question: How do I know when to send the proposal?
    answer: The nine-dimension Proposal Readiness Gate checks Problem Clarity, Business Importance, Solution Fit, Scope / Delivery Clarity, Economic Fit, Decision Authority, Stakeholder Clarity, Timing / Decision Process, and Mutual Commitment. Each condition is Clear, Unresolved, or Blocking. A proposal is justified only when no blocking condition remains and material uncertainty has been resolved or responsibly bounded.
  - question: Does this teach me how to write and negotiate the proposal?
    answer: No. This product deliberately stops at Proposal Ready. It teaches you how to mature the opportunity until proposal work is justified and what information should be handed forward. The Developer Proposal & Closing Kit handles final scope and acceptance criteria, final pricing and payment structure, the buyer-facing proposal, proposal review, downstream negotiation, agreement, payment, and the start gate.
  - question: Do I need the Freelance Developer Launch Kit first?
    answer: No. If you can already deliver a credible service and sales is the bottleneck you clearly need to fix, the Sales Playbook is designed to support a competent first execution on its own. Use the Launch Kit instead when several parts of the business are still unclear and you need the broader end-to-end operating system.
  - question: What happens when the prospect is not ready or not a fit?
    answer: The system does not force every conversation toward a proposal. Depending on the evidence, the responsible outcome may be more discovery, Paid Discovery, Reshape, Defer, Nurture, Refer, or Decline. A clean no can preserve time and margin better than a proposal built on weak fit.
  - question: Is AI required?
    answer: No. The core sales process is fully usable without AI. The optional AI Sales Assistant Prompt Pack can help organize, extract, and audit source material, but it is explicitly bounded from inventing buyer facts, making final recommendations, or replacing your judgment.
