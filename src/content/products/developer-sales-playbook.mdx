reframe: |-
  Developers are rewarded for being helpful, so a sales call can turn into free consulting almost without noticing. The prospect asks how you would build it. You explain the architecture, the stack, the tradeoffs, maybe even the solution. Forty-five minutes later they know more, you know the feature list, and nobody is much closer to deciding whether this project should happen.

  That is frustrating because the call did not feel bad. It felt productive. The missing information is usually commercial: why the problem matters now, what it is costing, who is involved, what could make the project a poor fit, what the buyer is comparing, and what has to be true for a decision to happen.

  The Developer Sales Playbook gives that conversation a spine. You prepare, diagnose before recommending, qualify the consequences and fit, understand the decision conditions, and end with an explicit next step or a clean no. You still get to be useful. You just stop confusing usefulness with progress toward a sale.

    steps:
      - label: Start with uncertainty, not the solution
        value: Summit serves multiple cities, has announced a second office, and asks about a site redo before fall. The developer deliberately keeps the real problem, economics, approval path, content readiness, and technical dependencies marked unknown instead of assuming the requested rebuild is justified.
      - label: Move beneath “outdated”
        value: Discovery establishes that marketing depends on the current agency to create location structures, location information is duplicated, campaign publishing is inconsistent, paid search is expanding, and the second-office launch creates a real timing trigger.       - label: Map diagnosis into a recommendation
        value: Confirmed needs become a structured CMS for location control, a campaign landing system for focused paid traffic, preservation of the working CRM handoff, and a phased launch for the September deadline. Each recommendation includes a practical effect and a tradeoff.
      - label: Diagnose resistance instead of rebutting it
        value: Summit says another proposal is almost $4,000 cheaper. The developer learns that $9,200 is affordable; the real issue is whether the difference over a $5,400 visual-refresh option is justified. Proposal Ready means the opportunity is ready to document commercial alignment, not that the buyer has already agreed to purchase.
    conclusion: The proof is cumulative reasoning. The problem discovered early in the conversation is the same problem summarized in diagnosis, used to justify the recommendation, tested during the objection, and scored again at Proposal Readiness.
