reframe: |-
  A successful project can leave something more valuable than a testimonial. The client may still have systems to maintain, planned improvements to make, campaigns to support, technical decisions to revisit, or responsibilities that are cheaper to keep with someone who already understands the work.

  That continuing need can become recurring revenue. Done well, it gives the client a dependable service and gives your business a portion of revenue that does not have to be replaced from zero every month.

  But recurring billing is not automatically a good retainer. “Ten hours a month plus support” sounds simple until urgent requests, unused time, vendor problems, new integrations, and quick changes start consuming a larger share of the calendar than the monthly fee can support. A predictable invoice can hide unpredictable economics.

  Freelancer Retainer System starts with the continuing client need, not the desire for a subscription. You decide what responsibility is worth owning, what stays project work, what rights the client receives, how requests move, what response expectations mean, what the full obligation costs, how much capacity is actually being reserved, and what evidence should change the service later.

  The result is recurring work that can strengthen the business instead of quietly turning one client into an employer. You get a service the client can understand, economics you can inspect, boundaries that protect other revenue, and a renewal process that can expand, resize, or end the relationship when the evidence says it should.

faqs:
  - question: Can I use this to turn clients I already have into retainers?
    answer: Yes. That is the strongest use case. Start with a current or former client where you can see legitimate work continuing after the original project. The system helps you identify which part of that demand belongs in a monthly service, which part should remain project work, and how to turn the recurring portion into an offer you can put in front of the client.
  - question: Do I need to already have a retainer before I use the product?
    answer: No. You can use it before you have ever sold a retainer, while you are preparing a recurring offer for an existing client, or to clean up a loose monthly arrangement you are already running. The guide starts with the decision of whether recurring service belongs at all and builds the service from there.
  - question: Will it tell me what to include in the retainer?
    answer: Yes. You work out what the client is actually paying you to own, which systems and obligations are covered, what work can be requested, who can request and approve it, how response expectations work, what the client is responsible for, and what triggers a separate project. You are not handed a canned package; you build the service around the client need you actually have.
  - question: Does it tell me what monthly price to charge?
    answer: >-
      It teaches you to calculate the full recurring burden and establish an internal economic floor before you make the commercial pricing decision. That includes more than coding time: recurring delivery, coordination, interruption, response burden, direct account costs, variability, and any real capacity commitment. It does not pretend there is one universal market rate that every freelancer should charge.
  - question: How does this help me avoid scope creep and always-on support?
    answer: The service is designed with boundaries before it is sold. You define client rights, eligible requests, response behavior, limits, exclusions, approval rules, project triggers, overage routes, and capacity guardrails so a new request has somewhere to go besides automatically becoming included work.
  - question: What exactly do I receive?
    answer: You receive the 168-page Freelancer Retainer System guide and the 138-page companion implementation workbook. The guide contains ten connected chapters and the workbook contains ten matched records that take one real client relationship from opportunity diagnosis through service design, economics, capacity, proposal, onboarding, monthly operation, exception handling, and renewal.
  - question: Is this only for website maintenance retainers?
    answer: No. The product teaches a broader recurring-service design method. Depending on the real client need, the service may center on maintenance, support, planned growth work, reserved capacity, managed responsibility, or a bounded combination. The same method can also tell you when the work should remain project-based instead.
  - question: What happens when a good retainer client wants more work?
    answer: You do not have to choose between saying yes to everything and holding the client at the original scope forever. The system shows you how to test whether the new demand belongs in the recurring service, requires a resize or restructure, or should be sold as separately approved project work. That gives the relationship room to grow without automatically giving away more permanent capacity.
  - question: Does this replace a contract or legal review?
    answer: No. The Retainer Operating Specification is the internal operating source of truth for the service, not a legal contract. The product is educational material and does not provide legal, tax, accounting, security, insurance, worker-classification, or jurisdiction-specific advice. Use the actual agreement and qualified professional review where those issues matter.
