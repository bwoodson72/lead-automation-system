reframe: |-
  A prospect finally gets on the call. They tell you what they want. They ask how you would build it, what stack you would use, how long it might take, and what something like that costs. You want to be helpful, so you start solving.

  The call can feel productive while the sale stays dangerously vague. You may know more about the feature list without knowing why the project matters now, who can approve it, what the buyer actually needs to change, which technical unknowns could wreck the estimate, whether the economics fit, or what the buyer will use to make the decision.

  Then comes the familiar request: "Can you send me a proposal?" You spend hours turning incomplete information into scope and price, send the document, and wait for the proposal to finish a sales process that never really happened.

  The Developer Sales Playbook replaces that improvisation with a decision process. You learn how to understand the buyer before you recommend, diagnose the problem without inventing pain or ROI, test mutual fit and commercial reality, connect the recommendation to confirmed needs, work through resistance without canned rebuttals, and decide whether the opportunity is actually ready for proposal work.

  The goal is not to force more prospects to say yes. It is to stop guessing what the conversation means. A strong sales process should tell you when to advance, when to investigate, when to reshape the opportunity, when paid discovery makes more sense, and when the right answer is no.

  system:
    title: Seven active stages from real conversation to Proposal Ready.
    intro: The playbook gives every live opportunity a current stage, required evidence, and a justified next decision. The sequence is structured enough to prevent omission without turning the conversation into a rigid script.
    steps:
      - Conversation · decide whether a real opportunity is worth exploring
      - Discovery · build evidence about the situation, problem, consequences, desired state, constraints, economics, stakeholders, and decision process
      - Diagnosed · confirm or correct a coherent explanation of what is actually happening
      - Qualified · decide whether the opportunity fits across problem, solution, economics, delivery, decision, timing, and relationship

      - Commercially Aligned · establish enough agreement about broad engagement shape, responsibilities, territory, timing, and conditions to test readiness
      - Proposal Ready · pass the nonnumeric readiness gate or take the responsible alternate disposition

faqs:
  - question: Is this a sales-script pack?
    answer: No. The playbook gives you structure for the decisions that matter, not lines to memorize. You learn what must be understood before you recommend, which evidence changes the answer, how to follow the buyer's response without losing the purpose of the conversation, and how to review your own behavior afterward.
  - question: Do I need to be naturally good at sales?
    answer: No. You do need to be willing to ask business questions instead of retreating into technical explanation. The system relies on preparation, evidence, diagnosis, fit, explicit next decisions, and reviewable behavior rather than charisma or pressure.
  - question: How do I know when to send the proposal?
    answer: The nine-dimension Proposal Readiness Gate checks Problem Clarity, Business Importance, Solution Fit, Scope / Delivery Clarity, Economic Fit, Decision Authority, Stakeholder Clarity, Timing / Decision Process, and Mutual Commitment. You use Clear, Unresolved, or Blocking rather than a score. A proposal is justified only when no blocking condition remains and any material uncertainty is resolved or responsibly bounded.
  - question: Does this teach me how to write and negotiate the proposal?
    answer: No. This product deliberately stops at Proposal Ready. It teaches you how to mature the opportunity until proposal work is justified and what information should be handed forward. The Developer Proposal & Closing Kit handles final scope and acceptance criteria, final pricing and payment structure, the buyer-facing proposal, proposal review, downstream negotiation, agreement, payment, and the start gate.
  - question: Do I need the Freelance Developer Launch Kit first?
    answer: No. If you can already deliver a credible service and your clear bottleneck is sales, the Sales Playbook is designed to support a competent first execution on its own. Use the Launch Kit instead when several parts of the business are still unclear and you need the broader end-to-end operating system.
  - question: What happens when the prospect is not ready or not a fit?
    answer: The system does not force every conversation toward a proposal. Depending on the evidence, the responsible outcome may be more discovery, Paid Discovery, Reshape, Defer, Nurture, Refer, or Decline. A clean no can be better sales execution than a proposal built on weak fit.
  - question: Is AI required?
    answer: No. The core sales process is fully usable without AI. The optional AI Sales Assistant Prompt Pack helps organize, extract, and audit source material, but it is explicitly bounded from inventing buyer facts, making final recommendations, or replacing your judgment.
  - question: What do I receive?
    answer: You receive the 353-page Developer Sales Playbook digital guide and the 195-page implementation workbook. The guide contains 15 chapters plus the AI Sales Assistant Prompt Pack, Glossary for First-Time Sellers, Five-Minute Sales Field Guide, Research Notes and Sources, and the guide/workbook crosswalk. The workbook contains 15 matching implementation modules plus 10 branching Practice and Reinforcement scenarios.
  - question: Will this help me close every qualified opportunity?
    answer: No. No sales process can control the buyer's decision. This product is designed to improve the quality of the decisions and evidence that lead to proposal work, including recognizing when an opportunity should not continue. It does not guarantee projects, close rates, revenue, or buyer agreement.
