reframe: |-
  “Send me a proposal” sounds like progress because the buyer is asking for something concrete. But if the important commercial decisions are still open, the proposal can become one of the most expensive pieces of unpaid work in the sale.

  You start estimating while writing. Scope is defined inside the document. Risks appear after the price is already visible. A buyer question becomes a casual scope change. A revision quietly changes the economics. A verbal yes creates excitement, but agreement, payment, access, or another stakeholder still prevents the project from starting cleanly.

  The issue is not proposal design. It is commercial continuity.

  By the time you build the buyer-facing document, the problem, desired state, recommendation, obligation, material uncertainty, price logic, payment structure, responsibilities, acceptance, and decision process should already be explicit enough that the proposal can record the deal instead of discovering it.

  Developer Proposal & Closing Kit teaches that controlled path. You gate readiness, decide what can responsibly be sold next, turn discovery into a commercial recommendation, bound the work, route unknowns, confirm terms, assemble the proposal from current records, structure the review, diagnose objections before changing the deal, version material revisions, cross a real start gate, and hand the accepted commercial state into delivery.

  The commercial benefit is simple: less wasted proposal effort, fewer late surprises, stronger protection of price and scope, and a clearer path from qualified opportunity to paid engagement.

faqs:
  - question: Is this a proposal template pack?

  - question: Does the kit include ready-made closing scripts?
    answer: No. The product explicitly favors responsive diagnosis over rigid rebuttal scripts. It teaches you to preserve the buyer's actual words, consider competing explanations for the objection, identify the real decision constraint, and then choose a response that fits the evidence.

    answer: No. It explains the commercial jobs different records can perform and helps you keep proposal, SOW, agreement, payment, and current-state records consistent. Contract language, legal effect, IP treatment, procurement terms, and jurisdiction-specific questions may require qualified legal review.

  - question: What happens if the buyer asks for changes after the proposal is sent?
    answer: The system separates clarification from a material commercial change. When scope, quantity, timing, payment, responsibility, risk, or another important term changes, you assess the downstream impact, assign the correct version state, update the source record, and reconcile the affected commercial documents before treating the deal as current.
