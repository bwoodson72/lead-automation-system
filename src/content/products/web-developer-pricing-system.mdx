  You finally get the kind of inquiry you wanted. The project sounds good. The client seems serious. Then they ask the question that can still make an experienced developer hesitate: “What would something like this cost?”

  Too low and you can spend the next eight weeks resenting a project that looked profitable on day one. Too high and you worry you just talked yourself out of the work. So you estimate the coding, add some padding, check what other freelancers charge, think about the client's budget, and choose a number you hope you can defend.

  A capable developer should not have to price serious work by anxiety. The Web Developer Pricing System starts with what your one-person business must earn, then accounts for the work clients do not see: discovery, meetings, research, project management, revisions, deployment, delays, support, uncertainty, risk, and sales time. You build rules for pricing models, payment, discounts, change requests, retainers, and minimum project size before negotiation pressure starts. The result is not one magic rate. It is knowing what the project has to be worth for the business to make sense.

faqs:
  - question: Does this tell me what hourly rate to charge?
    answer: It calculates an internal Minimum Sustainable Rate from your own economics. That gives you a floor for decision-making instead of a public rate copied from somebody whose costs, capacity, and business are different from yours.
  - question: Do I have to use fixed pricing?
    answer: No. Fixed price is useful when the conditions support it. The system also covers time and materials, paid discovery, retainers, and hybrid structures so uncertainty does not get hidden inside a confident-looking number.
  - question: What if I cannot estimate the project accurately yet?
    answer: That is not a personal failure. It is a commercial fact. Paid discovery, a capped diagnostic, time and materials, or a phased engagement can be safer than guessing a fixed price and hoping the unknowns behave.
  - question: Is this value pricing?
    answer: It is value-informed. Your economics establish sustainability, while problem importance, alternatives, specialization, proof, and business context can inform the commercial price. It does not ask you to invent revenue you do not control.
  - question: Will it help with discounts and extra work?
    answer: Yes. Discounts, revisions, change requests, add-ons, rush work, and payment timing become policies you decide while calm instead of awkward exceptions negotiated when you are afraid of losing the deal.
