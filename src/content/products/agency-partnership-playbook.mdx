reframe: |-
  Agency work has an obvious appeal. One good relationship can create several projects without making you find, educate, and close every end client yourself. When the pipeline has been unpredictable, “we can send you a lot of work” can sound like exactly the stability you have been missing.

  That promise becomes expensive when the details are vague. The agency expects a discount because volume might come later. You spend hours estimating work they have not sold. Scope arrives half-formed. Rush requests become normal. The agency owns the client relationship, so you have less control while one partner quietly becomes too large a share of your income.

  The Agency Partnership Playbook is about earning the upside without sleepwalking into the downside. You target agencies whose business model creates recurring technical demand, position yourself around the specific capacity problem you solve, qualify the economics and workflow, use a pilot to test the relationship, define scope and communication boundaries, and make good delivery visible enough to earn repeat work. The goal is not more agency logos. It is a small number of relationships that make the business stronger instead of more dependent.
