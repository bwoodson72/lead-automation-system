reframe: |-
  A prospect asks a simple question: “So what exactly do you do?” If the answer is React, APIs, integrations, automation, performance, custom development, and “it depends on what you need,” you have handed the buyer a job. They now have to translate your capabilities into a problem, an engagement, a scope, and a reason to choose you.

  That ambiguity follows you everywhere. Every inquiry becomes a fresh commercial design exercise. Scope changes with the person in front of you. Pricing has no stable object behind it. Delivery lessons are harder to reuse because the buyer, obligation, dependencies, and process all move at once.

  Productization does not mean forcing custom software into a fixed package. It means reducing avoidable ambiguity. You decide what should become stable, what can vary inside known limits, what must remain professional judgment, and what uncertainty needs discovery or a separate route before you promise implementation.

faqs:
  - question: Do I have to pick one niche forever?
    answer: No. Market focus is a testable commercial hypothesis, not a permanent identity. The system helps you make the buyer/problem specific enough to investigate, then narrow, broaden, change, or reject it when the evidence says you should.
  - question: What if my work is too unpredictable for a fixed package?
    answer: Then do not fake certainty. The product teaches you to separate the stable core from bounded variation, professional judgment, and material unknowns. Depending on the evidence, the right route may be productized implementation, productized discovery, a narrow component, custom work, or no offer yet.
  - question: Can I use this if I have little or no freelance client history?
    answer: Yes, if you can already deliver the technical work competently. You can use evidence from employment, agency work, subcontracting, open source, serious personal projects, demonstrations, and other experience you can accurately describe. The system will not let you invent buyer demand or client results you do not have.
  - question: Does this teach pricing?
    answer: It defines the commercial object that needs to be priced and records the uncertainty the pricing decision must account for. The Web Developer Pricing System is the separate system for economic floor, commercial price, pricing structure, and pricing decisions.
  - question: What if I do not have client case studies yet?
    answer: You can build honest proof from diagnostic insight, labeled demonstrations, inspectable artifacts, relevant real work, reviews you may truthfully use, and measured outcomes when you actually have them. Each proof type is limited to what it can legitimately demonstrate.
  - question: What exactly is in the workbook?
    answer: The 42-page workbook contains 11 matched worksheets. You build a Controlled Standardization Map, Capability & Repetition Map, buyer/problem evidence records, Productization Fit Review, Core Obligation, Qualification & Scope Boundary Record, Delivery System Map, Buyer-Facing Offer & Proof Plan, Minimum Offer Page & Test Log, Offer Version Review, and the final Offer Specification.
  - question: How do I know whether the offer is worth continuing with?
    answer: You do not decide from how polished the offer looks or from one enthusiastic conversation. The system helps you distinguish weak signals such as compliments from stronger evidence such as repeated problem recognition, meaningful next steps, commercial scope or price discussions, payment, successful bounded delivery, and repeat or adjacent demand. You then continue, modify, narrow, reroute, or reconsider based on the pattern.
  - question: Does this guarantee that my productized service will sell?
    answer: No. The product helps you build a defensible offer hypothesis, expose it to real buyers, and interpret the evidence. It cannot guarantee demand, clients, revenue, profitability, or that your first buyer/problem choice is correct.
