reframe: |-
  Technical capability is only one part of freelancing. You still have to decide what you will sell, who has a reason to buy it, what you are actually promising, whether the economics work, how buyers will find and trust you, when an opportunity is ready for a proposal, how the project starts, how changes are controlled, how completion is verified, and what the evidence says you should improve next.

  Most freelance advice isolates those decisions. Change your rate. Rewrite your website. Post more. Send outreach. Fix your proposal. Ask for retainers. Any one of those moves can be useful, but none of them can repair a broken dependency somewhere else in the system.

  The Freelance Developer Launch Kit builds the business in dependency order. You start with the commercial system itself, choose a testable market and paid obligation, make scope and economics observable, build honest proof and transaction readiness, create buyer access, diagnose opportunities, move only sufficiently understood work to a commercial decision, deliver under controlled risk, and then review the whole system against real evidence.

  The goal is not to make the business look finished. The goal is to make it operable. By the end of the first cycle, you should know what you currently sell, how the system works, what remains uncertain, what the evidence supports, and what constraint deserves attention next.

faqs:
  - question: Does the 90-day operating cycle guarantee clients or revenue?
    answer: No. Ninety days is a bounded management and evidence horizon. The goal is to finish with an operable freelance-business system and better evidence about what should change next. Buyer timing and purchase decisions are not under your control.
  - question: Do I need to finish the whole book before I contact buyers?
    answer: No. The system deliberately overlaps setup, market contact, selling, and learning. Early buyer evidence is supposed to change later decisions instead of arriving after you have spent months polishing assumptions in isolation.
  - question: What does the workbook actually contain?
    answer: The 77-page workbook contains 28 reader-facing worksheets, one for each guide chapter. Every worksheet includes a completed Alex Morgan / Summit Air & Heat example, a procedure and decision rule, and the reader's own operating record. Earlier outputs become inputs to later worksheets.
  - question: Why are there only 28 worksheets when older Launch Kit materials referred to LK-coded tools?
    answer: The current edition uses 28 chapter-matched reader-facing worksheets. Legacy LK codes remain only as internal migration references where useful. You do not need them to use the product.
  - question: How is this different from buying the focused Developer Business Lab products?
    answer: The Launch Kit is the minimum complete freelance-business operating system. The focused products deepen one subsystem after evidence shows that pricing, acquisition, sales, proposals, onboarding, delivery, proof, referrals, recurring work, or another area deserves more sophistication or practice.
  - question: Is this only for brand-new freelancers?
    answer: No. It also fits experienced developers whose independent business grew through referrals, marketplaces, subcontracting, or habit and now needs an explicit operating structure.
  - question: Does it teach retainers or recurring revenue?
    answer: It teaches you how to evaluate recurring work after delivery, but it does not force a retainer onto every client. Recurring work belongs in the business only when a genuine ongoing obligation, capacity need, support requirement, optimization need, or repeatable unit of value actually exists.
