reframe: |-
  A prospect asking for a proposal feels like progress. Then you open a blank document and realize you still have decisions to make. What exactly are you promising? What is excluded? Which unknowns can change the work? Who still needs to approve it? What does the price actually cover? What happens after you hit send?

  When those answers do not exist yet, proposal writing becomes unpaid commercial design. You make scope decisions while drafting. The buyer sees the price before the risk behind it is visible. A question in review becomes a casual scope change. A request to “adjust the proposal” quietly changes the economics. Even a verbal yes can leave you waiting on paperwork, payment, access, or another stakeholder before the project is actually ready to start.

  The problem is not that your proposal needs better formatting. The deal needs continuity. By the time you draft the buyer-facing document, the problem, desired state, commercial path, bounded obligation, material uncertainty, price input, payment logic, and decision process should already be explicit. Then the proposal can do its real job: make the current commercial decision understandable without inventing a new deal inside the document.

  The Developer Proposal & Closing Kit teaches that process as one connected operating system. You gate the opportunity, decide what can responsibly be sold next, turn discovery into a commercial recommendation, bound the work, route uncertainty, confirm terms, build the proposal, structure the review, diagnose objections before changing terms, control revisions, cross a real start gate, and hand the accepted commercial state into delivery.
