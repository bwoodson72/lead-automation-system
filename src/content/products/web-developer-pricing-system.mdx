reframe: >-
  The uncomfortable part of pricing is not usually saying the number. It is knowing whether the number makes sense. If you price from coding hours alone, you leave out discovery, meetings, research, revisions, project management, deployment, client delays, support, sales time, and technical uncertainty. If you price from what another freelancer charges, you inherit somebody else's economics. If you price from the client's budget, the buyer becomes the center of your business model. A defensible price needs a floor before it needs confidence. The Web Developer Pricing System starts with what your one-person business must earn, then works upward through total effort, uncertainty, risk, scope, value context, payment structure, and the pricing model that fits what is actually known. Fixed price is one option, not a rule. Paid discovery, time and materials, retainers, and hybrids all have a place when the conditions support them. The result is not one magic rate. It is a policy you can apply consistently and improve from completed-project evidence.
