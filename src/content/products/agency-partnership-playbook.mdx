reframe: |-
  Agency work solves a real problem: one partner can create several projects without forcing you to find and close every end client yourself. That can reduce acquisition effort and make planning easier. It can also make you accept terms you would reject from a direct client because future volume sounds valuable.

  The expensive parts often appear after the relationship feels promising. You start reviewing unsold projects for free. Incomplete designs become your estimating problem. Rush requests become normal. Client calls blur authority. Capacity gets held before demand is committed. One agency becomes large enough that saying no feels commercially dangerous.

  The right question is not whether an agency can send work. It is whether you can enter its delivery system without creating hidden economic, operating, legal, or concentration risk in your own business. The playbook uses evidence first, commitment second: form a demand hypothesis, qualify the prospect, discover the operating model, run a bounded paid pilot, measure actual delivery and cost to serve, then decide what kind of repeat relationship, capacity commitment, reset, or exit the evidence supports.

faqs:
  - question: Is this mainly an agency outreach guide?
    answer: No. Outreach is only the front end. The product covers the full relationship from pre-outreach qualification through pilot design, commercial terms, live delivery, repeat work, capacity, concentration, economics, reset, and exit.
  - question: Do I need previous agency experience?
    answer: No. You do need to be able to deliver professional client work reliably. The guide teaches the agency-specific business decisions from the beginning, including what you can know before contact and what must stay unknown until discovery or paid work.
  - question: Should I give agencies a lower rate because they can send volume?
    answer: Not automatically. Promised volume is not evidence. Different economics are justified only when the actual relationship changes your cost or risk in a measurable way, or when a real commercial commitment pays for reserved capacity.
  - question: Does it cover white-label and client-facing boundaries?
    answer: Yes. It covers who can make commercial commitments, how direct client questions are routed, portfolio and confidentiality considerations, scope authority, communication paths, and the need to keep the agency's client relationship clear without making your responsibilities vague.
  - question: What do I receive?
    answer: A 231-page guide and a 126-page implementation workbook. The guide contains 25 chapters; the workbook contains AP01 through AP25 so the decisions become a reusable operating record rather than notes you rebuild every project.
  - question: Does the 30-day cycle mean I should land an agency in 30 days?
    answer: No. Chapter 25 uses 30 days as a management window for the agency channel. It is not a promised sales cycle, reply deadline, pilot timeline, or closing target.
  - question: Does this replace legal review?
    answer: No. The playbook teaches commercial risk identification and operating decisions, but contract, IP, data, worker-classification, termination, and similar fact-specific issues may require qualified review.
